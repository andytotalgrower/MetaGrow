# Sample survey soil and tissue reports

Reports are available from **Samples → Soil and tissue reports**, the report icon on an imported soil survey, or **Report templates → View report** on the survey. Admin, Agriculture Manager and Agronomist can use reports. Template and report-definition maintenance retain their narrower Admin/Agriculture Manager permissions.

## Combined soil and tissue reports

Soil samples define the report's block list and order. Tissue results for blocks with no matched soil sample are excluded. Tissue-only surveys are outside this phase. Matching uses saved property block links and both existing lab-name mapping tables, with the existing conservative name/reference fallback and an audited manual confirmation for conflicting evidence. Banana and multi-crop block IDs remain separate namespaces. Multiple tissue samples on one block remain separate rows or row sets with their own sample identity; they are never averaged together.

Choose one soil template for the survey and a separate active Tissue template for each included survey crop. Selections use IDs; soil/tissue names do not need to agree. The soil sample's survey crop controls the tissue choice, not the lab's crop label. Where one block has soil samples for multiple crops, the tissue survey item's crop must identify the corresponding crop. The same template popup serves direct report links, Samples and CSV-created surveys. Tissue choices can be completed after import identifies the relevant crops. Changing a choice is audited in MetaGrow and invalidates an editable preview.

Tissue measurements are copied directly from laboratory data, preserving null versus measured zero. A matching target uses the existing test item's field binding and min/target/max, with below range red, within range green and above range amber. No tissue ratios, required amounts or product rates are calculated. Missing readings stay blank; unreported measures are omitted. Dynamic labels, order, formatting and visibility are maintained under **Administration → Soil and tissue report definitions**, using the converted `LabReportDefinitionTissue.xml` definition. Existing tissue targets continue to be maintained through the existing template manager.

**Tissue Nutrients** follows the soil measures inside each **Block Results** table, retaining the same four soil block columns. Spare space on the last soil page is used before creating a portrait continuation. Tissue parts and repeated samples retain separate row sets and their sample references; source parts and template names are available in tooltips rather than repeated beneath each block. Numerically identical tissue ranges share the Range column; differing ranges appear beside each result, regardless of template display names. A soil block without tissue sampling has blank tissue cells labelled **No tissue result**. Collected tissue awaiting laboratory results is identified separately; the report can be previewed but cannot proceed to peer QA until those relevant results arrive. Tissue-only blocks do not delay QA. Individual Block Detail pages include tissue in the same four-column layout as soil: measure, result, range and against-target-range diagram. Tissue diagrams use the selected tissue range and actual result, without adding a soil survey-average comparison. Continuations retain all applicable readings. Tissue rows containing only zero or missing values are omitted from the displayed table (including a single-sample detail table), regardless of ShowIfZero; mixed rows preserve individual zeroes. This presentation rule never alters frozen source values or audit history. All theoretical soil requirement pages still precede all soil balancing pages.

Soil and tissue share one agronomist review, preview, peer-QA revision and public share. Included tissue readings, targets, definitions and matching evidence are frozen with that revision. Changes to included readings/targets invalidate an editable preview; changes confined to excluded tissue-only blocks do not. A manual tissue match confirmer cannot approve the revision. Public shares retain tissue readings and ranges but omit internal match reasons and staff attribution. Existing approved soil-only reports remain frozen.

## Workflow

1. Choose one active soil calculation template on the Sample survey. If none is selected, the Samples report button opens a DevExpress popup before continuing; direct report links provide the same setup. Templates beginning with `x` are excluded. Every soil block uses that template; each survey sample's crop controls crop-specific rules and notes. Region selection is deferred: settings saves, CSV generation and calculations always use Default (1).
2. Import the complete soil result set. Other sample materials do not hold up the soil report. Each result must match one soil sample in the same survey and laboratory. The mapped property block is the primary match; a unique block name (case and whitespace ignored) is used where a block mapping is absent. References/barcodes support matching and distinguish multiple samples on a block, but may be absent or outdated. A reference that identifies a different sample, duplicate matches, or unmatched results opens **Match soil samples**. Missing results still prevent calculation. Completeness comes from the imported results, not the separate legacy laboratory workflow status: an older survey can still show **Sent by agronomist** after all results have arrived, as in survey 7208. That stale status does not block opening or QA and is not modified by the report.

   **Sample matches** also opens from the report toolbar. The agronomist can choose a compatible survey sample beside each laboratory result, supply a reason for manual choices, or restore automatic matching. Save and view report validates the whole set together; a sample can never be reused. Manual choices are stored only in MetaGrow with the confirmer, reason and a fingerprint of the paired records. Changed identifiers require reconfirmation; a changed source while the popup is open rejects the stale save. Choices, workflow version and audit entries commit together. Changing a saved preview returns it to Editing and calculation produces a new revision. Submitted/approved reports keep their existing frozen revision. A person who confirmed a manual match cannot approve that revision's peer QA. The audit window and calculation trace expose the matching method and evidence; public shares omit internal matching records and staff attribution.

   For survey items that were never collected, **Not sampled** allows an explicit report-only exclusion with a required reason. It is stored and audited in MetaGrow, appears in the report, and never edits the survey or lab data in totalgs. An item with a lab result cannot be excluded this way; a later import requires clearing its exclusion. All retained soil samples still require complete matching results. Exclusions are frozen during QA and approval.
3. Open the report. MetaGrow calculates from current laboratory results, template rules, product settings, regional limits and maintained report definitions. It appends a revision only when relevant inputs change.
4. **Agronomist review:** review the dynamic measures, theoretical elemental amounts, recommendations, automated notes and warnings. Use **Review actions and recommendations** to add key action groups, adjust products with reasons, exclude blocks with an audit reason, and select optional sections. **Save and preview** saves an immutable revision with status **Ready for QA** and returns to the full report. When only report sections change, the review note is optional: the server records which sections were enabled or disabled. Changes to actions, recommendations or included blocks still require a review note, as does confirming a review without changes. Review the calculated rates even when no adjustment is needed; record that in the review note.
5. Check the saved report, then **Submit for peer QA** with explicit preview confirmation. Editing cannot skip straight to QA. The preview confirmation and submission are audited separately in the same transaction. A second active agronomist approves the exact revision and records QA hours. The survey agronomist and anyone who edited or submitted that revision cannot approve it, including administrators. Confirmed email addresses are matched to active staff records. Changed inputs invalidate a saved preview on reopening and require another agronomist review; submission/approval also rechecks those inputs.
6. Approved reports can be shared through the existing expiring/revocable share mechanism. The link identifies the immutable approved revision. Returning a report to editing creates a new revision and does not alter an already shared approved revision.

CSV-generated soil surveys require a template before import. The successful import receipt supplies every newly created survey ID; MetaGrow then saves the chosen report settings for each. If that separate save fails, **Retry soil template setup** retries settings only and preserves completed imports and existing selections. Navigation warns about unfinished settings. A survey's settings can also be completed from its editor.

## Legacy calculation compatibility

The implementation follows `LabSoil`, `SoilTestTemplateTarget` and the relevant rendering calculations in `SoilReportPreviewHelper`. It avoids writing intermediate results to legacy tables. The effective rule order, conversion factors, percentage handling, product rounding, regional limits, cation-boron rules and crop adjustments are retained.

Known legacy behaviours are explicit rather than silently corrected:

- Missing lab fields use the legacy zero default for required-amount calculations, but remain blank in the displayed laboratory results and produce QA warnings.
- Missing/invalid legacy formula parameters retain the legacy zero required amount and produce a QA warning and trace entry. Unknown formula IDs and non-finite results fail calculation.
- The sugar nitric-potassium rule multiplies the band rate by block hectares before displaying kg/ha. This is retained for comparison with the legacy report and visibly flagged for agronomic review. A positive band rate requires a valid area. The instruction to ignore area is applied to historical averages.
- The calcium-over-75 rule can perform a second magnesium-oxide conversion after an earlier conversion. Its order is retained and recorded in the trace.
- The preview helper's low-TEC banana modifier changes freshly loaded template target objects, whereas its rendered recommendations read stored result rows. That apparently ineffective preview mutation is not introduced as a new numeric adjustment. Confirm this against representative legacy reports during acceptance testing.

The above behaviours need agronomic acceptance using real reports before production release. A visible warning does not itself make a recommendation correct.
